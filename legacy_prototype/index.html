This is an 11-month rental agreement for an apartment in Bangalore with a monthly rent of ₹32,000 and an upfront security deposit of ₹1,60,000 (5 months). The landlord permits termination with 1 month notice, but Clause 14 imposes an aggressive 50% forfeiture if you vacate before 6 months. Maintenance charges are excluded from rent.

Important Clauses Extracted

6 clauses audited
Clause 4 • Security Deposit Standard

"The Tenant shall pay a refundable interest-free deposit of ₹1,60,000/-, repayable upon handover of vacant possession minus legitimate utility dues."

Plain meaning: Landlord must refund full ₹1.6 Lakhs upon moving out. Deductions are only permitted for unpaid electricity/water or actual physical damage.
Clause 14 • Lock-in Period & Forfeiture High Risk / Unfavorable

"In the event the Tenant vacates the premises prior to 180 days, fifty percent (50%) of the total security deposit shall stand unconditionally forfeited as liquidated damages."

Plain meaning: If your job transfers you or you leave before 6 months, the owner claims a right to keep ₹80,000 automatically, which is often challenged under Indian Contract Act Section 74 unless actual loss is proven.
Clause 9 • Maintenance & Painting Charges Moderate

"Deduction of 1 month's rent (₹32,000/-) shall be made towards painting and sanitization at the time of tenancy termination."

Plain meaning: A fixed ₹32,000 painting fee will be subtracted irrespective of the actual wall condition when you leave.

Your Obligations

  • Pay Rent by the 5th: Monthly transfer of ₹32,000 with a late fee of ₹200/day after the 7th.
  • 1 Month Written Notice: Must notify landlord via email at least 30 calendar days before vacating.
  • Minor Repairs: You are liable for plumbing washers and bulb replacements up to ₹1,000.

Potential Inconsistencies & Red Flags

Notice Period Contradiction

Clause 7 mentions "Either party may terminate with 30 days notice", but Clause 14 enforces a mandatory 6-month non-refundable lock-in period.

Unilateral Landlord Inspection

Clause 11 grants landlord right of entry "at any hour without prior intimation", which infringes on tenant's right to peaceful possession.

Procedure for Claiming Refund from Seller on E-Commerce Platforms

1
Issue Formal Legal Notice to Seller

Before filing with the District Commission, send a formal 15-day notice detailing the purchase date, defect proof (photos/videos), and specifying that failure to refund will lead to forum filing with costs.

2
Lodge Grievance with National Consumer Helpline (NCH)

Call 1915 or register on consumerhelpline.gov.in. 60% of major retail and e-commerce disputes are resolved during NCH convergence without court visits.

3
File Case on e-Daakhil Portal

If unresolved, file an e-complaint on edaakhil.nic.in. Under Consumer Protection Act rules, claims up to ₹5,00,000 carry ₹0 court fee. You can appear in person via video conference.
